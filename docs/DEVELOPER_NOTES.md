- **Rules for every screen (owner's findings, 2026-10-29):**
  - **One scroll per area, never the window.** The sidebar and top bar stay fixed on desktop; the content area scrolls inside the app. On two-column screens (ticket page, new ticket, contact and company pages) each column scrolls on its own, so the ticket header, reply bar and Update stay in view. No screen shows two scrollbars for the same content. On phones the page scrolls normally under a fixed top bar and the bottom reply bar.
  - **Lists and tables scroll inside themselves.** On list screens (tickets, contacts, companies, members, teams, audit log, canned responses, fields, rules, SLA policies) the page title, filters and order stay in place; only the table body scrolls, inside its card, with the header row pinned at the top. A table wider than its card scrolls sideways inside the card, never the page. Paging and "load more" stay as they are. Tables inside another page (a contact's or company's tickets) follow the page's scroll unless they hold more than about 10 rows, then they get their own scroll with a pinned header.
  - **Every screen size, not only laptops.** Large desktop (1440 px and wider) and laptop (1024–1439 px): the fixed frame and inside scrolling above. Tablet (768–1023 px): the sidebar collapses to icons or opens as a menu, the top bar stays fixed, two-column screens stack (details under the conversation) and the reply bar and Update bar stay pinned at the bottom of the screen. Phone (below 768 px): one scroll for the whole screen under a fixed top bar, lists as cards (no scroll box inside a scrolling screen), filters in a bottom sheet, and the reply bar or Save bar fixed at the bottom. Every rebuilt screen is checked at 390, 768, 1280 and 1440 px wide, with long content, for: only one scrollbar per area, nothing cut off, and save buttons always visible.
  - **Thin scrollbars everywhere:** about 6 px, light grey (`#c9ced6`), darker on hover, rounded, no arrows (`scrollbar-width: thin` plus the WebKit rules), defined once in the global styles. Thin, not hidden: mouse users must still see and grab them.
  - **Never lose unsaved changes.** Anything saved with a button (ticket properties, settings pages, drawers) keeps that button in view: Update sits at the bottom of the properties panel, always visible; as soon as something changes, the bar turns brand-tinted with "1 unsaved change: Priority", changed fields are marked, and Discard appears next to Update. The same pattern applies on every screen size and every place that saves with a button: settings pages, drawers (rules, SLA policies, fields, teams, canned responses, invite), Your account and New ticket. Leaving the page, opening another ticket or closing a drawer with unsaved changes asks "You have unsaved changes. Leave without saving?". Saving each field on change (as Zoho Desk does) was considered and not chosen: it changes the flow, and each change would run rules and write history separately.

The owner approved these flows on 2026-10-28 (with the recommended answers to the open questions) and asked for Step 4 and then Step 5 to be built in a new session, one feature at a time as in Step 3: each through all six steps, committed and pushed to `dev-deploy` in both repos, marked "Waiting for review". **Step 4, SLAs.** Use case: an urgent ticket needs a reply within 1 hour; the team lead is alerted at 50 minutes; at 1 hour it is marked overdue and reassigned.

- **F22 Business hours and holidays** (Settings → Business hours, admins): one schedule per workspace in its time zone, working days with start and end times or 24×7; a list of holidays (date and name) entered by hand. Hours per team: Release 2.
- **F23 SLA policies and countdowns** (Settings → SLAs, admins): per priority, a first-reply time and a resolve time, each in business hours or round-the-clock hours. Starting values, editable: Urgent 1 h / 4 h, High 4 h / 1 day, Medium 8 h / 3 days, Low 1 day / 5 days (business hours). Extra policies with conditions (the rule condition builder), first match wins, otherwise the default. First reply is met by a reply the customer sees (not a note). The clock pauses only on Waiting on customer (Pending still counts), stops on Resolved and starts again on reopen. A priority change works the due times out again from creation. Tickets show "Reply due in 42m" / "Overdue by 10m"; the list gets an Overdue filter and a Due soonest sort.
- **F24 Warning and escalation** (part of each policy): a warning some minutes before the deadline to the assignee (team leads when unassigned); at the deadline, mark overdue and tell the team leads, and optionally reassign (to a team lead, a chosen person, or back to the team). A worker job checks every minute; each warning and escalation happens once per ticket.
- **F25 Notification center:** a bell with the unread count; items: assigned to you, @mentions, SLA warnings and breaches, customer replies on your tickets. Each person chooses which kinds also come by email; in-app is always on. It checks every minute until F26.

**Step 5, Agent experience.**

- **F26 Live updates** (Socket.IO with the PostgreSQL adapter, no Redis): the server sends only short signals ("ticket 42 changed") to rooms per workspace, ticket and person, and the browser reloads that part through the REST API, so permissions and workspace separation stay in one place. The ticket list shows "3 new tickets · Show" instead of moving rows; an open ticket shows new messages, property changes and history at once; the bell updates at once (replacing the one-minute check); worker changes (email tickets, rules, escalations) show too. The connection uses the session, is cut on sign-out or deactivation, reloads what is on screen after reconnecting, and falls back to checking every minute when blocked.
- **F27 Collision detection:** "Also viewing: Neha" and "Neha is replying…" (replies and forwards, not notes), gone about 30 seconds after she leaves; before sending, a warning if someone else replied since the ticket was opened ("Neha replied 2 minutes ago": Review or Send anyway), not a block. The existing "Someone else changed this ticket" check stays.
- **F28 Merge and split:** merge (tickets.merge: admins and supervisors) 2 to 5 tickets into a chosen main one: messages, notes, files and tags move in, in time order, marked "From #41"; the others are closed, read-only and "Merged into #42"; their requesters are added as Cc on the main ticket; replies to their old email threads land on the main ticket; telling customers is a checkbox, off by default; no undo, a clear confirmation, recorded in the audit log. Split (any agent) a customer message into a new ticket with its files and the same requester, "Split from #42" / "Split into #57" in both histories; the new ticket runs the "ticket created" rules. End-to-end tests use two signed-in browsers at once.
- **F29 Home page** (added 2026-10-28 at the owner's request; the full dashboard stays first in Release 2): for admins a "Get started" checklist (invite your team, connect your support email, set your business hours), each linked and ticked when done (someone joined or was invited, a ticket came in by email, the hours were saved once), gone when all are done; for everyone four cards, Assigned to you, Overdue, Unassigned and Waiting on customer, each a count that opens the matching ticket list, updated live and every minute.
